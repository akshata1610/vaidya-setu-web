# India Teleconsultation Market Research

Prepared for Akshata Anil Urs · 7 November 2026

Vaidya Setu addresses the explanation and review of instructions following a recorded consultation. Published utilisation supports investigating this channel. It does not establish demand, willingness to pay or clinical benefit for this MVP.

## 1 Scale is established; the figures count consultations

**50 crore+ — cumulative eSanjeevani consultations · 15 Sep 2026**

The 6 October 2026 MoHFW release records 50,00,92,730 teleconsultations across 36 states and union territories. It reports approximately 2.5–3 lakh consultations per day, 2,41,923 registered providers, more than 1.42 lakh spokes, 19,473 hubs and 901 online OPDs. These are programme statistics, not unique patients, paid transactions or Vaidya Setu's addressable market. [Source: MoHFW / PIB · 6 Oct 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2319427&lang=1&reg=48)

**Product implication to validate:** Test a post-consultation instruction workflow with providers; a large programme does not establish demand, purchasing authority or willingness to pay for this product.

## 2 Assisted telemedicine deserves its own workflow

**>93% — provider-assisted consultations · Jan 2020–Sep 2023 study**

A descriptive study using C-DAC programme data reports more than 163 million consultations during January 2020–September 2023, with over 93% delivered through the assisted AB-HWC model. It reports that five states accounted for approximately 70.6% of consultations. For 2022, 108,840 of 139,389 registered AB-HWCs were operational on the platform; Karnataka's table lists 8,065 of 9,310. These historical programme measures do not describe the current private market. [Source: Adoption and utilization of India's eSanjeevani national telemedicine service · programme study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12558045/)

**Product implication to validate:** Interview the health worker or clinician who could review the output, as well as the patient. Direct-to-consumer and assisted workflows have different upload, consent and delivery needs.

## 3 Older users and caregivers matter in the research

**40.15% — eSanjeevani consultations among people aged 46+**

The October 2026 release reports women account for 57.14% of utilisation and people aged 60+ account for 16.25% of consultations. The 46+ category already includes those aged 60+; the two percentages must not be added. Consultation shares are not a profile of unique customers. [Source: MoHFW / PIB · 6 Oct 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2319427&lang=1&reg=48)

**Product implication to validate:** Include older patients, women and caregivers in discovery. Test replay, audibility, number comprehension and shared-phone access rather than treating smartphone access as independence.

## 4 A website is feasible; assisted access remains important

**85.5% — Indian households with a smartphone · Jan–Mar 2025**

MoSPI's survey of 34,950 households reports 86.3% have internet within the premises. Smartphone possession was 82.1% in rural households and 91.3% in urban households; internet access was 83.3% and 91.6%, respectively. Among rural people aged 15+, reported internet use in the previous three months was 72.1% for males and 57.6% for females. Household possession, individual ownership and ability to upload a file are different measures. ICT skills were self-reported rather than tested. [Source: MoSPI / PIB · 29 May 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2132330&lang=2&reg=48)

**Product implication to validate:** Use a responsive browser workflow with clear upload states. Test on shared phones and slower networks; interview people outside the digitally confident sample. These data do not measure WhatsApp use or preference for audio.

## 5 Private-platform activity needs separate denominators

**7.99 million — Apollo 24/7 digital consultations · calendar 2025**

Apollo Hospitals' Health of the Nation 2026 report attributes 7.99 million digital consultations, 8.5 million lab bookings and 197,000 psychiatry consultations to Apollo 24/7 in calendar 2025; it states 25% of users are aged 55+. These are company-reported platform measures, not independent national market totals. Lab bookings are not consultations, and 55+ is not the same age band as eSanjeevani's 46+ measure. [Source: Apollo Hospitals · Health of the Nation 2026, calendar-2025 platform data](https://www.apollohospitals.com/sites/default/files/2026-04/hon-report-apollo--web-2026.pdf)

**Product implication to validate:** Provider-owned digital channels are another discovery segment. Do not add these counts to government totals to estimate unique Indian users or assume access to their recordings.

## 6 Digital-health revenue is broader than teleconsultation

**₹234 crore — Practo company-reported FY25 revenue**

Practo's FY25 recap reports more than ₹3,500 crore GMV, ₹234 crore revenue and ₹15 crore operating EBITDA. Its business includes care navigation and other services; the post does not provide a standalone Indian teleconsultation revenue total. GMV is the value of transactions and is not the company's revenue. [Source: Practo · FY25 recap (company report)](https://blog.practo.com/fy25-recap-a-year-of-profitability-progress-and-purpose/)

**Product implication to validate:** Avoid deriving a teleconsultation TAM, a revenue multiple or a price for Vaidya Setu from these figures. Establish the buyer, eligible consultations, actual processing cost and acceptable review effort separately.

## 7 Recording review is a separate step from practising telemedicine

**2 models — assisted doctor-to-doctor and direct patient-to-doctor**

C-DAC describes both assisted and direct-to-patient eSanjeevani variants. The MoHFW telemedicine guideline document identifies context, clinician/patient identity, communication mode, consent, consultation type, evaluation and management as core considerations. This research summary does not establish legal compliance or authorise independent prescribing. [Source: C-DAC platform description; MoHFW Telemedicine Practice Guidelines](https://www.cdac.gov.in/index.aspx?id=product_details&productId=eSanjeevaniNationalTelemedicineService) [MoHFW guideline document](https://www.mohfw.gov.in/pdf/Telemedicine.pdf)

**Product implication to validate:** Position this build as a reviewed explanation of a clinician's stated instructions. Obtain specific permission for recording, external AI processing and archiving; keep sign-in and case ownership checks. Production clinical deployment needs a separate review of applicable requirements.

## 8 Discovery questions before a pilot

These are proposed research questions, not findings from completed interviews.

| Participant | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| Clinician / clinic operator | Who can approve a translated explanation? Which errors would stop delivery? How much checking is acceptable? |
| Assisted teleconsultation worker | Who records and uploads? Who owns the phone? How would consent and recipient checks work? |
| Patient / caregiver | Can they explain the dose, timing and duration back after listening? Can they find and replay the audio? |
| Provider buyer | Which consultations are eligible? What data controls, integration, evidence and budget would a pilot require? |

## 9 Product and commercial evidence still missing

A pilot should establish recording permission, who reviews instructions, the approved recipient workflow, actual API/review cost, language preferences, field errors, spoken-output fidelity, review burden and patient teach-back. No number of paying clinics, annual eligible consultations, conversion rate, price or market share is assumed. A small role-play exercise is not a representative clinical benchmark. The website archives original drafts and separately verified references so these observations can be collected without rewriting history.
